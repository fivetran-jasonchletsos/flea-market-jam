Photos and Pricing

Photos

  • Use daylight from a window, or a lightbox. Turn the flash off to avoid glare.
  • Use a plain background. A white sheet shows the true color of cobalt glass. A dark background shows shape and clarity.
  • Take the full item first, then every mark, the base, any flaw, and a shot with a ruler or coin for scale.
  • For jewelry, take close-ups of each stamp and the clasp. Hold the camera steady and tap to focus.
  • Keep your house out of the frame. Photos you publish on this site are public.

Pricing from sold listings

  1. Search for the item on eBay with its maker, pattern and form.
  2. Filter to Sold items. Asking prices do not matter. Sold prices do.
  3. Match condition and details. A chipped piece is not a comparison for a perfect one.
  4. Throw out the highest and lowest prices.
  5. Take the typical price. To sell faster, price a little under it.
  6. Write down your comparisons so you can explain the price later.

eBay Seller Hub has a Research tab (called Product Research) with several years of sold data. See eBay's page on it.

Adding fees to your price

Work backward from what you want to keep. If you want to net 50 dollars on eBay in a typical category, fees take roughly 14 percent, so list near 59 dollars before shipping. On Marketplace local pickup, fees are zero, but buyers haggle, so leave 10 to 20 percent of room.

Using the scanner price

The scanner gives a range, a quick-sale price and a confidence level. It is an AI estimate from general knowledge, not live sales data. Use the search phrase and the links it gives you to check the range yourself every time.
