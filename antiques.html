Identifying Antiques

Age clues

One clue is not a date. Put several together to get a range.

  • Dovetails. Hand-cut dovetails have uneven spacing and small layout marks, and the tails are often about twice as wide as the pins. Machine-cut dovetails are even and uniform. Machine cutting spread in the late 1800s, so hand-cut joints suggest earlier work but do not prove it.
  • Saw marks. Straight, uneven marks suggest an early saw. Curved arcs suggest a circular saw, which came in during the 1800s.
  • Screws and hardware. Uneven threads and off-center slots suggest hand-made screws. Even threads and pointed tips are later.
  • Finish and wear. Wear should fall where a piece was used, such as feet, arms and drawer edges. A uniform shiny finish may be recent.
  • Style alone is not a date. A style can be reproduced many times. A design from 1760 could be built in 1900 or 1980.

Furniture style names

  • Victorian. Roughly 1837 to 1901. Heavy, ornate and carved.
  • Renaissance Revival. About the 1850s to 1880s. Architectural shapes, carving and panels.
  • Mission and Arts and Crafts. About 1900 to the 1920s. Plain, straight, often oak.
  • Art Deco. About the 1920s to 1940s. Geometric and streamlined.

These are loose labels. Treat any date as a range and say "attributed" or "circa" when unsure.

Pottery

American makers such as Roseville, Hull, McCoy and Weller are collected, and lookalikes and fakes exist. Photograph the base, any mark, and any numbers. Match them to the maker's collector club or a trusted mark guide before you name the maker.

Silver plate or sterling

Sterling is marked sterling or 925. Plated pieces are often marked EPNS, plate or quadruple plate, and may show copper or brass where worn. Test before you decide, and do not scrub tarnish off an unknown piece first.

Research the maker and the price

  1. Search the exact mark plus the type of item.
  2. Check museum collections and collector clubs.
  3. Value from sold prices, not asking prices. See Photos and Pricing.
  4. Match condition. Cracks, repairs and missing parts change the price.
  5. Keep a note of your comparison for each item.

What not to refinish

  • Do not strip, repaint or heavily sand the original finish on period furniture. The original surface is part of the value.
  • Do not polish unknown metals, grind glass or scrub pottery glaze.
  • When unsure, sell as found and describe the condition honestly. Dust it gently and leave it.

Bulky items

A carved oak bench or chest is heavy. List it on Marketplace for local pickup, measure it, and photograph the joints, the carving and the underside.
