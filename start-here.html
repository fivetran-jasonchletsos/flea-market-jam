Start Here: How Reselling Works

Every sale follows the same loop. The scanner on this site helps with steps 2 and 3, and the lessons cover the rest.

  1. Find. Estate sales, thrift stores, yard sales and your own closets. Buy only what you can sell for at least twice what you paid, after fees.
  2. Identify. What is it, who made it, how old is it, and is it real? Look for marks first.
  3. Price. Look at what the same item actually sold for, not what people are asking.
  4. Photograph. Good photos sell items. See Photos and Pricing.
  5. List. Pick the platform that fits the item. See the eBay, Etsy and Marketplace lessons.
  6. Ship or hand off. Pack fragile things well, or sell locally and skip shipping. See Shipping and Safety.
  7. Learn. Write down what sold, how fast and for how much. That record is how you get better.

Which platform for which item

ItemUsually bestWhy
Cobalt glass, rare or higher valueeBayCollectors search nationally and you can see sold prices.
Cobalt glass, common piecesMarketplace, local pickupShipping and breakage can cost more than the profit.
Jewelry over about 50 dollarseBay or EtsyWider audience. Etsy suits vintage-style pieces.
Costume jewelry, low valueMarketplace or bundlesFees take a big share of small sales.
Small antiques and decoreBay or EtsyEtsy buyers look for vintage and gift items.
Furniture and heavy itemsMarketplace, local pickupNo fees and no freight.

This table is practical advice from common reseller experience, not a platform rule.

Do not list the same item everywhere at once

If it sells on one site and is still listed on another, you must cancel a sale or refund an angry buyer. Until you have a system to delist, list each item in one place.
