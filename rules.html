Rules: What You Cannot Sell or Claim

Penalties for these can be serious. When an item might be covered, do not list it until you have checked the current federal and state rules, or pass it to a licensed dealer or auction house.

Antique and vintage

By common convention, antique means 100 years or older and vintage means at least about 20 years old. These are trade conventions. Etsy defines vintage as 20 years or older. The 100-year mark also matters in law for some protected materials. Do not write antique unless you have evidence of the age.

Ivory

  • Federal law sharply limits selling elephant ivory and other protected ivory. An antique exemption exists, but it has several conditions, including an age of at least 100 years, no repair or change with the material after 1973, and, for some items, entry through a designated port.
  • State laws can be stricter, and a number of states restrict ivory sales.
  • Etsy and other sites restrict ivory listings even when an item is old.
  • Carved pieces, piano keys and scrimshaw are common sources of trouble. Unless you have documents and have checked the rules, do not list them. Bone, resin and celluloid are often mistaken for ivory.

See the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service pages on ivory, and a summary of its antique rules on ITSArtLaw.

Tortoiseshell

Hawksbill turtle shell is protected internationally, and selling it is restricted. Combs, boxes and eyeglass frames sold as tortoiseshell are high risk. Much of what is called tortoiseshell is plastic or celluloid. If you cannot tell, do not call it real tortoiseshell.

Feathers

Federal law bars selling eagle feathers and parts whatever their age. Most native wild bird feathers are also protected. Leave feathers out of your listings. See an example enforcement case on the Fish and Wildlife Service site.

Native American items

The Indian Arts and Crafts Act is a truth-in-advertising law. It prohibits misrepresenting a product as Native American-made, or as made by a particular tribe. Only call an item Native American if you have documents showing it. Avoid wording such as "Navajo style" on pieces you cannot verify. See the Fish and Wildlife Service page.

Firearms and ammunition

Federal law treats guns made in or before 1898 as antique firearms, and generally does not regulate them like modern guns. States can differ, and most selling sites do not allow firearm listings at all. Do not list guns, ammunition or parts. Take them to a licensed dealer or auction house. See the ATF page on antique firearms.

Glass with uranium or lead

Tell buyers if a piece is uranium glass, and do not sell glass of unknown type as food-safe. Some sites have rules on radioactive material. Read the site's policy first.
